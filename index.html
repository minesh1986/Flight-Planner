Before anything else

Getting the weather

You need it twice — departure now, destination in the cruise. Three sources:

  1. The tablet. Weather or METAR app, type the ICAO, done.
  2. Your SimBrief OFP. METAR and TAF for both airports printed at the bottom. Ages, but fine for planning.
  3. ATIS on the radio. MSFS shows you the frequency when you select the airport.

How to read one Example only

This is a made-up sample to show you the format. The numbers below are not live — this file has no weather feed. Get the real report from the tablet, your OFP, or the ATIS, then type its wind into the box at the top of the page.

EIDW 241650Z 05008KT CAVOK 21/10 Q1019
05008KTWind from 050° at 8 knots. First three digits are the direction, last two the speed. Type your real one into the box at the top as 050/08.
Q1019QNH 1019 hectopascals. This is your altimeter setting. Typically 980–1040 — if the real report reads Q1002, you dial 1002.
CAVOKNothing to worry about. If it reads something like 3000 BKN008, that's 3 km visibility and broken cloud at 800 ft.

Two numbers matter: the wind picks your runway, the Q number goes in the altimeter. Everything else is background.

Stage 01

SimBrief

  1. Aircraft type B738. Not B737, not B38M.
  2. Route EGSS → EIDW. Leave alternate blank and let SimBrief pick.
  3. Tick "Detailed Navlog". Without it the route arrives red in the FMC and won't load. Most common failure there is.
  4. Cruise level — SimBrief will suggest one. For this sector FL300 is realistic. If SimBrief picks much higher on a short leg, lower it, or the FMC throws UNABLE CRZ ALT and VNAV drops out.
  5. Cost index — Ryanair run about 6, Jet2 similar. SimBrief's default is higher.
  6. Press Generate OFP. The aircraft pulls the most recently generated plan, not a saved draft.
  7. Note your SimBrief username — the login name, not the 6-digit Pilot ID.

Stage 02

Cold and dark — powering up

Load at a stand, not a runway. Any remote or terminal stand works; the sim doesn't care which, and pushback works from all of them.

  1. Battery ON Overhead — bottom left, electrical panel. Screens flicker into life.
  2. Standby power to AUTO Overhead.
  3. Connect the GPU from the tablet's ground services, or go straight to the APU.
  4. APU: switch to START, hold, release to ON. Wait for the APU GEN light. Then APU GEN 1 and 2 ON.
  5. Fuel pumps ON Overhead — all six, unless the centre tank is empty in which case leave the centre pair off.
  6. Packs AUTO, bleeds ON, isolation valve AUTO Overhead.
  7. Nav and logo lights ON, cockpit lighting to taste.
  8. IRS alignment: both IRS switches to NAV Overhead. It takes several minutes — start it early and do the FMC while it aligns.
Order that saves time Power, IRS to NAV, then straight to the tablet and the FMC. The alignment runs in the background while you're programming, so it's finished by the time you need it.
Stage 03

Tablet

It's on the left sidewall by the captain's seat. Runs on its own battery, so it works before you have power.

  1. First time only: settings/gear icon → enter your SimBrief username.
  2. Request New Data from SimBrief. Takes 10–20 seconds — it's simulating a datalink.
  3. Weight and Balance page → Request Weights. This loads passengers and cargo.
  4. Load the fuel from the same page. Then check the gauges actually moved.
  5. Note the CG figure off that page. You'll need it on TAKEOFF REF.
Don't Never touch the MSFS weight & balance menu once the tablet has loaded. The two systems don't talk and you'll end up with the FMC and the aircraft disagreeing about your weight.

EZFW vs ZFW won't match — that's normal. EZFW is SimBrief's estimate, ZFW is the actual. Use the actual in the FMC.

Stage 04

Programming the FMC

4a · IDENT

INIT REF. Check it says 737-800, note the AIRAC date. ROUTE at 6R.

4b · RTE

  1. Origin EGSS at 1L, destination EIDW at 1R.
  2. FLT NO at 2R — your flight number, e.g. FR202.
  3. Route request prompt at 6R. Pick the SimBrief route — it should be green. Red means Detailed Navlog wasn't ticked.
  4. LOAD, then ACTIVATE, then EXEC.
  5. Press NEXT PAGE to see RTE 2 — that's your VIA/TO airways, filled in by the uplink. You never type these.

4c · DEP/ARR — the only manual part

Press DEP ARR. Departure and arrival procedures aren't in the route file because ATC assigns them on the day.

  1. DEP: pick your runway first, then the SID. Which SID? It's on your SimBrief navlog, first line. Match the name.
  2. ARR: pick the STAR from your navlog, then the ILS for your landing runway, then a transition if one is offered.
  3. Names may differ by a digit. Your OFP might say BAGS4L and the FMC offers BAGS3L. Same procedure, different AIRAC. Take what the FMC has.
  4. EXEC after each change.

4d · LEGS — clean up

  1. Discontinuities — a line of boxes. Press the LSK next to the waypoint below the gap, then the LSK on the boxes. Gap closes. EXEC.
  2. Silly distances. Your total is 240 nm, so a 3,000 nm leg means a duplicate waypoint on the wrong continent.
  3. Altitude constraints in small font next to waypoints are legitimate — they come from the SID and STAR. Leave them.

4e · PERF INIT

INIT REF → PERF INIT REQUEST, or type it in.

FieldEnter
ZFWThe actual figure from Weight and Balance
FUELLeave it — reads from the tanks
RESERVESFrom the OFP. Don't leave it at zero or the fuel predictions mean nothing
COST INDEX6 for Ryanair-realistic
CRZ ALTFL300 — five digits or FL-prefixed. Typing 300 means 300 feet
UNABLE CRZ ALT If this appears, the FMC can't build a profile at that level for this distance and weight. Lower the cruise altitude and re-EXEC. Ignore it and VNAV drops out — which takes LNAV with it and leaves you hand-flying with no descent planning.

4f · N1 LIMIT

Accept the default, or pick 26K/24K for a reduced-thrust takeoff. Normal on a light short sector. TAKEOFF at 6R.

4g · TAKEOFF REF

  1. FLAPS — type 5. Standard 737-800 setting.
  2. CG — from the tablet's Weight and Balance page.
  3. Only then do V1, VR and V2 appear on the right in small font. Press each LSK to accept. They go large.

V-speeds blank? Flaps and CG aren't in. Every single time, no other cause.

Stage 05

Cockpit setup

Altimeters Glareshield

EFIS control panel at each outboard end. BARO knob with STD button in the middle. Press toggles standard; turn sets the value.

  1. Check units are hPa, not inches. There's an IN/HPA selector on the same panel.
  2. Set the departure QNH from the METAR Q number.
  3. Both sides plus the standby altimeter Forward panel, the small round dial centre panel.
  4. Sanity check: on the ground you should read about 348 ft. Wildly different means the setting is wrong.

Pressurisation Overhead

  • Mode selector AUTO
  • FLT ALT — 30000, your cruise altitude in feet
  • LAND ALT — 250, destination elevation to the nearest 50

Minimums Glareshield

Same EFIS panel, MINS knob with RADIO/BARO selector. Set BARO and dial 450 — roughly 200 ft above destination elevation, the standard ILS decision height.

  2. Heading — runway heading as a backup if LNAV drops.

  • Speedbrake DOWN Pedestal — not armed for takeoff

Stage 06

Start, taxi, takeoff

  1. Beacon ON before anything turns.
  2. Packs OFF, APU bleed ON. Engines and packs want the same air and there isn't enough for both.
  3. Engine 2 first: start switch to GRD. At 25% N2, start lever to IDLE. Fuel flow, then N1, then EGT.
  4. Then engine 1.
  5. After both: generators on, APU bleed off, APU off, packs AUTO, probe heat on.
  6. Flight controls full travel both ways.
Before Taxi
Before Takeoff

The roll

  1. Lined up, brakes released. Advance to about 40% N1 and pause — both engines stable and matched.
  2. Press TO/GA — the buttons on the front face of the thrust levers, where your thumb sits.
  3. Hand stays on the levers until V1.
  4. V1 — hand off. VR — rotate to 15°.
  5. Positive rate — gear up.
  6. 400 ft minimum for the autopilot. CMD A. 1,000 ft is more comfortable.
  7. LNAV engages at 50 ft, VNAV at 400 ft. Watch them go green.

Stage 07

Climb

  1. Wind the MCP altitude up as you approach each level, eventually to 30000.
  2. At the transition altitude — 6,000 ft in the UK — press STD on both EFIS panels and set 1013 on the standby.
  3. Passing 10,000 ft — landing lights off. VNAV releases the 250 kt limit itself.
If VNAV won't climb Check the FMA. ALT green means you've hit the MCP altitude — wind it up. If VNAV has dropped out entirely, press LVL CHG with about 280 in the speed window and climb manually. LNAV keeps flying the route regardless.
Stage 08

Descent — the bit that catches people

The 3:1 rule

Altitude in thousands × 3 = miles needed. Add about 10 miles for slowing down. From FL300 that's 100 nm.

Run this in your head every few minutes in the cruise. When it disagrees with the FMC, trust the arithmetic and start down.

Your descent profile

  1. About 10 minutes before top of descent, do the whole arrival prep in one block: destination METAR, load the approach on DEP/ARR, set the MCP course, autobrake to 2, get your VREF.
  2. Then wind the MCP altitude down to 3,000 — last action, and your cue that everything else is done.
  3. VNAV starts the descent itself at top of descent. You press nothing. Watch for the thrust coming back and the nose lowering.
  4. If nothing happens, VNAV isn't engaged. Press LVL CHG, 280 in the speed window, and fly the table above manually.
  5. Descending through the transition level, press STD off and set the destination QNH — both sides and the standby.
  6. Passing 10,000 — landing lights on, slow to 250.
The trap TO T/D changing to TO E/O on the PROGRESS page means the FMC no longer has a descent point ahead of you. Nothing will start a descent automatically. Go to LVL CHG immediately and fly the table.
Stage 09

Approach

Setup, done in the cruise

  1. Destination METAR — wind for the runway, Q number for the altimeter.
  2. DEP ARR → ARR → STAR, then the ILS for your runway, then a transition if offered. EXEC.
  3. LEGS check for gaps where the STAR meets the approach.
  4. NAV RAD — confirm the ILS frequency and course auto-tuned. If blank, type them in. A blank NAV RAD page is a silent failure: APP will do nothing and there's no warning.
  5. MCP course to the runway inbound — about 284.
  6. APPROACH REF — select the flaps 30 VREF. Around 140 kt at typical landing weight. Fly VREF+5.
  7. Autobrake 2, landing altitude confirmed.

Intercepting

Three conditions before APP:

  1. Level at 3,000 by 15 nm. Give yourself margin — 12 nm is tight.
  2. At or below the glideslope. The 737 cannot capture from above. Fly level and let the beam come down to you.
  3. Roughly aligned, within about 30° of the runway course.

Speed first, then flaps. Set the speed, wait for the aircraft to slow, then move the lever. And you can't slow down and descend at the same time — that's why being level at 3,000 early matters so much.

Gear at 8 nm is your anchor. Late gear is the single most common reason approaches arrive too fast.

Landing

Stabilised by 1,000 ft: on speed, on path, configured, spooled. If not — go around.

Stage 10

Landing

Autoland

  1. After APP is armed, press CMD B. The second autopilot only engages in approach mode.
  2. FMA must read LAND 3 or LAND 2. NO AUTOLAND means fly it yourself.
  3. 1,500 ft RA — FLARE arms white. 50 ft — FLARE green, nose comes up. 27 ft — autothrottle retards.
  4. Touchdown — ROLLOUT engages, speedbrakes fire, autobrake works. Reverse thrust is manual.
  5. Disconnect at taxi speed, before turning off the runway.

Needs a CAT II/III equipped runway. Not every destination has one.

Manual from 500 ft

  1. Fly the ILS on one autopilot to 500 ft.
  2. Disconnect with the yoke button. Press again to silence the warning.
  3. Leave the autothrottle in to hold your speed while you fly.
  4. At 30 ft, flare to about 5° and close the thrust levers.
  5. Touch down, lower the nose, reverse, let the autobrake work.
After Landing

Go-around

TO/GA twice → Flaps 15 → positive rate, gear up → climb to the go-around altitude and clean up.

Reference

The words

CDUThe keypad-and-screen box on the pedestal. FMC is the computer behind it — people use both words for the same thing.
LSKLine Select Key. Six buttons down each side of the CDU screen, numbered 1L–6L and 1R–6R from the top. Unlabelled — they do whatever the line beside them says.
ScratchpadThe bottom line of the CDU screen. Everything you type lands there first, then you press an LSK to put it into a field. Type it, then place it.
Small vs large fontSmall is the computer's suggestion, large is confirmed. Some things — V-speeds especially — don't apply until you press the LSK to promote them.
EXECCommits pending changes. If the light is on, something isn't live yet. Press it.
VIA / TOVIA is the airway, TO is where you leave it. The SimBrief uplink fills both in — you never type these.
SIDStandard Instrument Departure — a published route out of an airport.
STARStandard Terminal Arrival Route — the same thing arriving.
OFPOperational Flight Plan. The document SimBrief generates.
ICAO codeThe four-letter airport code (EGSS). Different from the three-letter one on a boarding pass (STN).
ZFWZero Fuel Weight — aircraft plus passengers plus bags, before fuel. EZFW is SimBrief's estimate; they won't match and that's normal. Use the actual.
V1Decision speed. Below it you stop, above it you go. Hand comes off the thrust levers.
VRRotate. Pull back to about 15° nose up.
V2Takeoff safety speed — guarantees a climb on one engine. You'll exceed it easily on two.
VREFLanding reference speed for a given flap setting. Fly VREF+5 on final.
N1Fan speed as a percentage — the closest thing to a direct thrust reading. N2 is the core; that's what you watch to 25% on start.
QNHLocal pressure setting, the Q number on a METAR. Set it and your altimeter reads height above sea level.

Setting up the displays

All of this is on the EFIS control panel Glareshield — the same one with the BARO and MINS knobs.

A few degrees down in the cruise.
Reference

Camera views on Xbox

The CDU is unusable from the default seat position. Build these once and they're saved per aircraft.

  1. Bind the commands. Options → Controls → Xbox Controller (duplicate the preset first if it's locked) → filter for custom camera. Bind Load Custom Camera 1–4 to LB + D-pad, and Save Custom Camera 1–4 to something awkward like RB + D-pad.
  2. Bind cockpit translate. Filter the Camera (Cockpit) category for Translate. Looking turns your head; translating slides it. Without this you can never get square-on to a panel. Bind the axis versions if you have a spare hat — much smoother than stepping.
  3. Also bind Reset Cockpit View. You'll wander off somewhere daft and want to snap back.
  4. Save four views: CDU · MCP and PFD · overhead · throttle quadrant. Add the EFIS panel and the tablet if you have slots spare.
  5. Must be in the normal cockpit camera to save — not drone, showcase or external.
  6. Test them on approach, not just parked. A view that's fine on stand can hide something you need in flight.

Quickviews on the D-pad already exist by default — eight fixed directions from the pilot seat. Useful for glancing, but they won't zoom you onto the CDU.

If ATC is reading out something enormous You've set both a callsign and a flight number and it's saying both. Clear one. Real ATC only ever uses the callsign — the flight number is for passengers and paperwork. FlightRadar24 is the easy way to get a genuine one: look up the route, copy the callsign, registration and the cruise level they actually use.

Distances are great-circle — your SimBrief route will be slightly longer because airways don't fly straight. Use it for the descent maths, not for fuel.

Runway headings are derived from the designator and accurate to a few degrees. Read the exact ILS course off the ND or the approach chart once the approach is loaded.

Transition altitudes vary by country and airport. Where one isn't shown, read it off the ATIS or the chart.

SIDs, STARs, ILS frequencies and gates aren't listed here — they change every AIRAC cycle. SimBrief gives you the procedure names; the FMC and the ND give you the frequencies.
